# Evidence quality and methodological limits of DTW on financial time series

Reading status legend: FULL = I extracted and read the full text (pdftotext) or most of it. ABS = abstract or publisher/repository summary only. SNIP = only a search-result snippet or a search-tool summary (weakest, unverified). "Reasoning" labels my own inferences, not source statements. Searches were few (about 15 tool calls), so absence of evidence below means "not found", not "does not exist".

## 1. Which studies compare DTW directly with correlation, Euclidean or other baselines on financial data, and what did they find (incl. null or negative results)?

### Takeaway
The direct comparisons are few, mostly small, and mostly about cluster-quality indices or lead-lag trading, not about out-of-sample usefulness. The one cost-aware, causally validated test I found (an unpublished master's thesis) is essentially null for DTW features out of sample. The one top-tier-group paper (Oxford) reports DTW-based lead-lag beating a cross-correlation baseline, but with modest Sharpe ratios, no transaction costs, and DTW-vs-other-clustering differences that are small.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, Cucuringu, Shestopaloff, Zohren (arXiv 2309.08800, q-fin.ST, Sept 2023; ICAICF'23 per search summary, venue not confirmed by me). FULL text read. Method: DTW + k-medoids to detect lead-lag in a lagged multi-factor model, sliding window length l = 21 days, then trade "leaders" to predict "laggers". Baseline: sample cross-correlation function (CCF) lead-lag matrix, plus four non-DTW clustering algorithms (KM/SP Mod/Med). Reported equity-dataset Sharpe ratios (rescaled to target vol 15%, table "Dα strategy"): CCF 0.675 vs best DTW variant 0.934 ("DTW KMed Med"); in the other table CCF 0.595 vs DTW 0.77 to 0.847 (non-DTW KM/SP variants 0.69 to 0.86 in the same tables, so some non-DTW clustering baselines are close to DTW). Authors state PnL is "excluding transaction costs". The text itself notes that after 2008 "except for CCF, all algorithms" grew similarly and that before 2008 DTW variants led on one strategy; i.e. the DTW edge over the other clustering baselines is period-dependent. Authors also state window size S "is of critical importance" in simulation. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08800v1.pdf)
- Singh, "Enhanced Time Series Analysis and Trading Strategy Development Using Dynamic Time Warping", Master's thesis, Politecnico di Torino, March 2026 (not peer reviewed; the author declares limited generative-AI use). FULL text read in large part. Design: DTW "pattern-outcome" features (similar historical windows, their subsequent returns) added to technical indicators, S&P 500 daily log returns, window z-normalisation, fixed Sakoe-Chiba band (radius 3 in the parameter list), features at time t use only data up to t, one-shot test after validation-only selection. Results: validation AUC 0.5178 (tech only) vs 0.5240 / 0.5266 (DTW / tech+DTW); test AUC 0.4936 (tech only), 0.5072 to 0.5081 (DTW only), 0.5034 (tech+DTW), all "close to 0.5". Frozen strategy net Sharpe on the test set 0.2619 at 5 bps cost, max drawdown -0.1879. Walk-forward: 38 windows (750-day train, 125-day test), mean net Sharpe 0.702, std 1.273, with positive and negative windows. Zero-shot on GLD: net Sharpe tech-only 0.4294, tech+DTW 0.3808, DTW-only 0.1290, max drawdown for DTW-only -0.7026, higher turnover. Author's conclusion: DTW features "do not produce consistent enhancements in out-of-sample performance on average", signal is regime-dependent and does not transfer across assets without recalibration. — [thesis PDF](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf)
- Cheong et al., "Comparative analysis of time series clustering: DTW and Euclidean distance measure in price index of companies in the S&P 500", 11th Malaysia Statistics Conference, 19 Sept 2024 (conference paper). FULL text read. Data: Kaggle S&P 500 closes 2013-02-08 to 2018-02-08, 35 firms dropped for unequal length, prices rebased to index 100, then SMA-10 smoothing, k-means. Rand Index / Davies-Bouldin: DTW-average (k=3) RI 1.00, DBI 0.46; DTW-median (k=4) RI 0.83, DBI 1.23; Euclidean (k=4) RI 0.96, DBI 0.87. Authors pick DTW-average. Silhouette suggested k=2 for all three and was dismissed by the authors as "overly simplistic". Clusters compared to GICS sectors with a chi-square test (statistic 96.85). — [paper PDF](https://www.dosm.gov.my/uploads/files/mystats-conference/2024/scientific-papers/5-Paper-Comparative-Analysis-of-Time-Series-Clustering-Dynamic-Time-Warping-and-Euclidean-Distance-Measure-In-Price-Index.pdf)
- Tsinaslanidis, Alexandridis, Zapranis, Livanis, "Dynamic Time Warping as a Similarity Measure: Applications in Finance", Hellenic Finance and Accounting Association conference, Dec 2014. ABS only. Simulation shows the DTW-based similarity rho_DTW is lower when Pearson/Spearman are high in absolute value and higher when they are low; applied to 8 indices, similarity rose in high-volatility periods; April showed higher similarity. — [Kent repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/43498)
- Journal of Statistical Computation and Simulation, 2012 (DOI 10.1080/00949655.2012.678361): compares Euclidean and DTW dissimilarities with MDS on 15 financial indices (daily, 10 years, plus 1-year tranches), and questions an earlier correlation-based study (Machado et al.). SNIP only (publisher returned 403; authors not confirmed by me). — [DOI link](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/00949655.2012.678361)
- Kim et al., "Pattern Matching Trading System Based on the Dynamic Time Warping Algorithm", Sustainability 10(12), 2018 (KOSPI 200 futures, morning pattern determines afternoon exit, 13 and 27 representative patterns). ABS only: the abstract reports no returns, costs or out-of-sample figures; claims "stable and effective" strategies. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v10y2018i12p4641-d188468.html)
- Other cluster-quality DTW-vs-Euclidean studies (Indonesian stock theses/articles, silhouette-based): SNIP only, via search summary; they report DTW giving better silhouette. I did not open them. They use cluster-quality indices, not any economic outcome.
- I found no study comparing DTW clustering against correlation-distance clustering on the same stock universe with an economic out-of-sample criterion (search-tool summary also said it found none). This is a not-found, not a proof of absence.

### Inferences (reasoning)
- Cluster-index wins (RI, DBI, silhouette) measure geometric agreement with the chosen distance, not usefulness for portfolio, risk or prediction. Note the Malaysian paper's own silhouette result (k=2) contradicts the k it ends up using, and RI there is computed between clusterings, so RI = 1.00 is not an accuracy against ground truth (the paper describes RI as comparing clustering results). Treat as weak evidence.
- Tsinaslanidis et al. suggest DTW similarity is largely a monotone-ish function of correlation-type information; if so, DTW may add less than its flexibility implies. This is from an abstract of a conference paper: weak.
- The only null/negative economic result found is the Politecnico thesis, which is itself unreviewed, single-asset (SPX plus GLD), and daily horizon.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed null/negative DTW-in-finance paper found. Not checked: Journal of Financial Data Science SMU paper (see Q4), Tsinaslanidis & Guijarro 2021 (chart patterns, not DTW; SNIP only).
- Zhang et al. full tables for ETF/futures and transaction-cost robustness not examined beyond the equity tables.

## 2. Known pitfalls

### Takeaway
Several pitfalls are structural to DTW (full-path alignment uses both series' whole windows; warping window is a free parameter that strongly affects results). Others are generic to finance pattern-matching (non-stationarity, multiple testing, costs). Only some have been tested for DTW specifically. I found no DTW-in-finance paper quantifying look-ahead leakage from non-causal alignment.

### Cited Findings
- Look-ahead and causality. In the thesis every feature at time t uses only data up to t and the label is the next return (explicit "strict causality by construction"); the author states that rigorous causality is a main message. Position at t-1 earns return t, costs charged on position change. This is a design choice, not evidence about how much leakage a non-causal DTW would create. — [thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf)
- Zhang et al. use a rolling window l = 21 and forecast with past data (EWMA of past seven days' returns of the "leaders") — the lead-lag is estimated per rolling window on past data, and DTW aligns inside the window. (FULL read of the strategy section; I did not audit the code, so I cannot confirm absence of leakage.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08800v1.pdf)
- Warping window. Ratanamahatana and Keogh, "Everything you know about Dynamic Time Warping is wrong" (UCR; I read the PDF, FULL; the SDM 2005 version is titled "Three myths about DTW data mining", which I did not read): the common 10% Sakoe-Chiba constraint "is actually too large for real world data mining"; classification accuracy usually peaks at small windows (their Fig. 5 and Table 2); "narrow constraints are necessary for accurate DTW". Data are classification benchmarks, not finance. — [PDF](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/DTW_myths.pdf)
- Window choice in finance specifically. Zhang et al. simulation shows ARI and MSE of lead-lag recovery depend on S; too-small S fails to capture the lag, so S must be at least the true lag (their synthetic data). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08800v1.pdf)
- Prices vs returns, normalisation. Studies differ: Cheong et al. use rebased prices (index 100) plus SMA-10 smoothing, with the smoothing window chosen by RMSE on a forecasting split (the smoothing before clustering uses the whole 5-year sample, so the grouping is in-sample and descriptive). Zhang et al. and the thesis use log/excess returns. The thesis z-normalises each window. I found no source that directly tests prices-vs-returns for DTW. — [Cheong](https://www.dosm.gov.my/uploads/files/mystats-conference/2024/scientific-papers/5-Paper-Comparative-Analysis-of-Time-Series-Clustering-Dynamic-Time-Warping-and-Euclidean-Distance-Measure-In-Price-Index.pdf), [thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf)
- Non-stationarity / regime dependence: thesis finds DTW features more useful in low-volatility regimes, weaker in high-vol, with strongly time-varying walk-forward Sharpe (std 1.273 vs mean 0.702 across 38 windows). Zhang et al. report DTW gains over baselines varying before/after 2008. — [thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08800v1.pdf)
- Transaction costs. Thesis models 5 and 10 bps candidates (5 bps selected on validation) and reports net Sharpe; Zhang et al. report PnL excluding costs. Kim et al. abstract gives no cost information. Tsinaslanidis & Guijarro (2021, chart-pattern recognition, 560 NYSE stocks, not DTW) say costs reduce profitability and control data snooping; SNIP only. — [thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf), [Tsinaslanidis & Guijarro PDF](https://riunet.upv.es/handle/10251/185285)
- Multiple testing in the tuned pipeline. The thesis selects among rule candidates on validation and then tests once; the validation net Sharpe 0.7433 for the chosen rule falls to 0.2619 on test (both from the thesis tables). That drop is the in-sample-selection effect in practice, though a single test cannot separate it from regime change. Zhang et al. test Sharpe significance with a p-value (their Appendix, a Sharpe-ratio test citing Ledoit-Wolf and Bailey-Lopez de Prado deflated Sharpe in references); the reported p-values are 0 or near 0, but the paper itself notes many tuning settings in the supplement. — [thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08800v1.pdf)

### Inferences (reasoning)
- Matching different dates / loss of simultaneity: DTW by design pairs x_i with y_j for i != j, so the output is a statement about shape under time deformation, not about co-movement on the same date. For risk (covariance, hedging, diversification) the same-date pairing is what matters, so a DTW distance has no direct risk interpretation. This is my reasoning from the definition; I found no finance paper that states or tests it directly (the Franses-Wiemann abstract, see below, embraces time-shifted similarity for business cycles, which is a different use).
- "Aligning in real time": a full DTW path over [t-l, t] uses the whole window, so it is only usable at the window's end; any path point other than the last one is "known" only because later points exist. It is not look-ahead if the window ends at decision time and the signal is acted on after t, but it becomes look-ahead if alignment is computed on the full sample and then used to label earlier dates (e.g. clusters computed on the full history used to form portfolios inside that history). That second form seems to be the case for the descriptive clustering studies (Cheong et al.), which is fine for description but not for any backtest. Reasoning, not sourced.
- Price levels are non-stationary and near unit-root, so similarity of rebased price paths is dominated by trend and common market factor (reasoning; the classical spurious-regression point of Granger and Newbold is not something I re-read). Cheong et al.'s own centroids are all upward trending, "high, moderate, low return" clusters, which fits this interpretation.
- Window z-normalisation removes level and scale, so DTW compares shapes; this discards volatility information that matters in finance (reasoning).

### Gaps
- No source found that measures the size of look-ahead leakage from non-causal DTW, nor compares prices vs returns inputs, nor studies DTW distance stability across window length on financial data.
- Franses & Wiemann (Computational Economics 56, 2020, DOI 10.1007/s10614-020-09986-0) was seen only via abstract/search summary: states DTW can capture alternating lead and lag among US state GDP series. Not read in full. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10614-020-09986-0/fulltext.html)

## 3. Wider literature on spurious pattern matching and validity of clustering financial series

### Takeaway
Keogh and Lin is directly relevant only to sliding-window subsequence clustering (and it uses S&P 500 data), not to whole-series clustering or DTW itself. Harvey-Liu-Zhu and Bailey et al. bear on any DTW strategy that is tuned over many parameters, but I read only abstracts, so I cannot say what they directly say about pattern matching.

### Cited Findings
- Keogh and Lin, "Clustering of Time Series Subsequences is Meaningless: Implications for Previous and Future Research" (ICDM 2003; KAIS journal version DOI 10.1007/s10115-004-0172-7). FULL text of the UCR PDF read. Claim: clusters from sliding-window subsequence clustering are "essentially random". Their experiment uses S&P 500 closing values and random-walk data: k-means cluster centres on the stock series "are not significantly more similar to each other than they are to cluster centers taken from random walk data". Whole-series clustering does not have the problem, and they say the difference is "due exclusively to the feature extraction step". Cluster centres degenerate to out-of-phase sine waves for small step sizes. They also note that weekly windows with different offsets ("Monday to Sunday" vs "Tuesday to Monday") give completely different clusters. DTW is only mentioned in a list of alternative distances; the paper does not evaluate it. — [Keogh & Lin PDF](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/meaningless.pdf)
- Harvey, Liu, Zhu, "... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", NBER WP 20592 (2014), Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 5-68 (2016). ABS only (NBER page). Multiple-testing framework; new factors should clear a t-ratio of about 3.0; "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false". — [NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)
- Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado, Zhu, "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance", Notices of the AMS 61(5), 458-471, May 2014. SNIP/ABS-level only. The more configurations are tried, the higher the probability of backtest overfit, and few researchers report how many were tried. — [WMU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/)

### Inferences (reasoning)
- Relevance test: the Keogh-Lin result applies only if a DTW study clusters overlapping sliding windows of a single series (e.g. "pattern types" of prices). It does not apply to clustering N different stocks as whole series (Cheong et al.) or to pairwise lead-lag in a window (Zhang et al.), though it is a caution against reading cluster centroids from windows as market "patterns". Whole-series DTW clustering has its own validity question: nothing in the literature I found tests whether clusters beat a random-walk or shuffled-surrogate null.
- Harvey-Liu-Zhu and Bailey et al. are about factor and backtest search. They apply to DTW pipelines because DTW adds free parameters (window, normalisation, k, K neighbours, thresholds). The thesis and Zhang et al. each tune many settings. The number of configurations tried is reported in neither of the two papers that I could verify (Zhang et al. refer to a supplement; not read).
- Not included: other time-series data-mining critiques (not finance-specific).

### Gaps
- Did not read Harvey-Liu-Zhu or Bailey et al. full text, nor Bailey and Lopez de Prado's deflated Sharpe paper (cited in Zhang et al.'s reference list). No source found applying these corrections to DTW specifically.

## 4. Do practitioners use DTW in production?

### Takeaway
I found no evidence of production use by an asset manager or hedge fund. All sources found are academic or student work.

### Cited Findings
- A Journal of Financial Data Science paper from Singapore Management University (2021) applies hierarchical clustering with DTW to group stocks by trend shape for allocation, rebalancing and stock selection. SNIP only (the repository page returned empty content); title, authors and results not confirmed by me. — [SMU repository](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/7238)
- Other found items are Indonesian and Italian academic papers on DTW clustering for portfolio building (SNIP only) and a Korean index-futures pattern-matching system proposal (Kim et al. 2018, ABS). None reports live trading. — [Kim et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v10y2018i12p4641-d188468.html)

### Inferences (reasoning)
- Absence of production evidence is weak evidence of absence: firms rarely publish methods, and my search was shallow (no practitioner blogs, conference talks, patents, job postings checked).

### Gaps
- Not searched adequately: SSRN, Journal of Portfolio Management, practitioner blogs, vendor documentation.

## 5. Overall: most and least defensible finance uses of DTW

### Takeaway
(All of this section is my reasoning from the sources above, not a source's statement.) Most defensible: descriptive, exploratory analysis of shape similarity where time shifts are the point (e.g. business-cycle or lead-lag timing, Franses-Wiemann type), and lead-lag detection with a causal rolling window, cost-aware evaluation and a correlation baseline (Zhang et al. design, though costs are missing there). Weakest: claims that DTW clusters of prices are "better" because a cluster index improves, DTW pattern-matching trading signals with many tuned parameters and no multiple-testing correction, and any use where same-date co-movement (risk, hedging) is the target.

### Cited Findings (summary pointers)
- Best-documented positive result: DTW lead-lag Sharpe above CCF in an Oxford study, ex costs — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08800v1.pdf).
- Best-documented negative or null result: out-of-sample AUC about 0.5 and weak, unstable net Sharpe for DTW features on SPX, no transfer to GLD — [thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/39690/1/tesi.pdf) (unreviewed).
- Cluster-quality wins over Euclidean exist but are small-sample and non-economic — [Cheong et al.](https://www.dosm.gov.my/uploads/files/mystats-conference/2024/scientific-papers/5-Paper-Comparative-Analysis-of-Time-Series-Clustering-Dynamic-Time-Warping-and-Euclidean-Distance-Measure-In-Price-Index.pdf).

### Inferences
- A fair statement for a course: DTW in finance is "plausible and occasionally profitable in published backtests, not established as superior to simple baselines, and with at least three free choices (window, input transform, normalisation) that change results".
- Suggested caution wording: describe any DTW-vs-correlation comparison on financial data as an illustration of what a choice does, not evidence of performance (consistent with the project's fixture rule).

### Gaps
- Verification still needed before citing in a lesson (project review gate): the JSCS 2012 paper (authors, findings; full text blocked), the SMU JFDS paper, Tsinaslanidis & Guijarro 2021, Franses & Wiemann 2020, the full Harvey-Liu-Zhu and Bailey et al. texts, and Zhang et al.'s published (ICAIF) version and supplement. The Politecnico thesis is a thesis, not peer-reviewed, and should be cited as such or not at all.
