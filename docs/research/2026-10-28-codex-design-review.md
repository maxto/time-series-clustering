**1. Builder check: questions that materially change the plan, ranked**

1. **What exactly is the finance companion's clustering task, and must it use real historical data?** (§5.3, lines 153–161.) Is an object an asset observed over a shared date window, a window from one asset, or a multivariate market episode? Is the purpose contemporaneous co-movement, similar volatility dynamics, or recurring regimes? These require different fixtures, representations, validation and explanations. Requiring real data also adds source, adjustment, calendar, universe and redistribution work. “Finance with caveats” does not choose a task. The exact instruments, dates and plotting details can remain with the builder once this boundary is set.

2. **Does runnable Python/R parity cover only the seven explicitly listed implementations, or also the methods delegated to packages?** (§7, lines 199–205.) “Everything else through packages” can mean a documented availability survey, selected runnable adapters, or executable coverage for every named measure. The latter substantially expands dependencies and conflicts with concept-only deep learning. The previous review did not establish maintained R counterparts for every advanced Python route. State which package-backed examples are required for release and whether an unavailable counterpart may remain a documented gap. The builder can select individual libraries after that decision.

I would not ask the owner to choose math tooling, SVG tooling, lesson lengths, exact subsection splits or test frameworks. Section 10 delegates those adequately. Licence approval and remote-action approval are already explicit later decisions, not missing answers that prevent drafting a build plan.

**2. Adversarial review**

**A. HIGH · WRONG: the finance contrast prejudges the answer.** (§5.3, lines 158–160.) “Representations that can work” versus shape matching that “does not” is too categorical. Correlation itself compares normalized synchronous trajectories: for centered unit-norm vectors, `||u-v||² = 2(1-r)`. Thus “correlation works, shape matching does not” is not even a clean mathematical distinction. Correlation-based financial grouping has a concrete precedent in [Mantegna's synchronous-return study](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9802256), which does not establish general predictive usefulness. Replace the contrast with a task-specific failure: shifting or warping returns can erase the simultaneity needed to study contemporaneous co-movement. Evaluate volatility/model features against their own stated objective. Neither visually attractive clusters nor a list of financial caveats establishes usefulness.

**B. HIGH · PARTLY CORRECT: geometry is the right organising principle, but silhouette does not accept arbitrary matrices.** (§5.2, lines 138–141.) Specify the course's input contract: finite, nonnegative, symmetric dissimilarities, zero diagonal, `2 ≤ k ≤ n−1`, and explicit singleton/zero-denominator handling. Triangle inequality is not required. A kernel similarity, raw soft-DTW loss, asymmetric alignment cost or matrix containing missing values must not silently enter that function. [R silhouette contract](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/cluster/html/silhouette.html), [scikit-learn precomputed input](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.silhouette_samples.html). Raw soft-DTW's negative values and self-comparison issues were already established in the previous review's [divergence paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/blondel21a.html).

Keep the CH/DB warning, but qualify it as the **standard Euclidean vector/centroid formulations**. Passing rows of a DTW matrix as feature vectors evaluates a new distance-profile geometry, not the original DTW geometry. Do not imply that any vector space with some centroid automatically satisfies those formulations. [Definitions and limitations](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/clustering.html#clustering-performance-evaluation).

**C. MEDIUM · INCOMPLETE: compatible scores are not automatically comparable or meaningful.** (§5.2 and lessons 12–13.) Explicitly distinguish three questions: can this index be computed, what geometry does it assess, and does that geometry answer the domain question? A higher silhouette under one distance does not certify that distance over another. ARI/NMI compare partitions of the same objects and can also compare repeated clusterings without ground-truth classes. NMI is not chance-adjusted. [Partition comparison documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/clustering.html#clustering-performance-evaluation). For the two electricity questions, use different declared reference partitions if external labels are shown. Explain what stability perturbs, preserve temporal dependence where relevant, and make clear that stable groups can still be irrelevant. For density methods, declare how noise points enter or are excluded from evaluation.

**D. MEDIUM · KEEP the electricity example, relax “every lesson”.** (§5.3, lines 146–152.) Twelve synthetic profiles are an excellent explanatory fixture. They are not evidence that a family performs well, nor a credible training set for the learned-representation lesson. Keep one base family and introduce controlled variants separately. Missingness should be a deliberate failure/preprocessing demonstration, not unexplained `NaN` support in every distance. Do not force alignment and a pairwise distance matrix into model-mixture or embedding explanations where those stages are absent. Use the relevant parts of the pipeline. Reserve the four-panel overview for places where it explains a complete comparison, with a simpler mechanism figure elsewhere. Otherwise the “every lesson” requirement conflicts with low-density mobile visuals.

**E. MEDIUM · ACCEPT the 15-lesson structure, bound the depth within it.** (§5.4.) Thirteen concept lessons plus two language labs is reasonable. The pressure comes from lessons 6, 7, 10 and 11, each containing enough material for several lessons. Give each one a single learning outcome and a worked representative, with contrasts and pointers for the others. The owner has already delegated exact splits, so no further decision is needed. The two validity lessons deserve their allocation. The earlier review's KASBA/2026 update has disappeared from the outline: retain a short sourced note in lesson 11 or the atlas so the course does not freeze centroid methods at DBA/k-Shape. A full implementation is unnecessary.

“Formulas live in optional measure cards” needs an editorial distinction: detailed derivations can be optional, but the local cost, normalization, DTW path constraints and meaning of a validation score must be understandable in the main lesson. Otherwise readers cannot make the decisions promised in §1.

**F. HIGH · PARTIAL pilot: good editorial sample, insufficient coverage of the riskiest additions.** (§9, lines 221–225.) Keep lessons 1 and 5 and the overview graphic. Within that pilot, include one representative finance note, one measure card with rendered math, and one short validity example distinguishing an admissible input from an inadmissible one. These are specimens, not three additional completed lessons. They test the newly adopted content and its rendering before the rest is authored.

Also move the **small numerical foundation** forward: save the pilot fixtures, hand-check a tiny DTW result, and compare the corresponding Python/R kernel before producing the definitive DTW figures. Complete the labs later. Otherwise “figures use the same fixtures as reference code” can still mean that prose and plots freeze around numerical conventions that the final code cannot reproduce. Shared inputs alone do not establish correct outputs, and two translations can share the same error.

**G. MEDIUM · ENGINE effort is understated, but these are builder tasks.** (§4 and §10.) The inspected engine is a fixed lesson-schema renderer rather than a general Markdown engine. Its `site/template.html:316` escapes text and implements bold only, and `scripts/build.py:140` defines fixed fields. Budget explicit support for reference links, math/cards, optional finance notes and readable code blocks. `scripts/build.py:24` restricts SVG elements/attributes, so generated scientific SVGs need a deliberate compatible export path. Prove that path during the pilot, including light/dark and mobile presentation. Decide deliberately how to retain or remove the engine's mandatory quizzes rather than inheriting them accidentally.

One concrete reuse hazard: `site/template.html:297` stores progress under `aicourse:`. Both GitHub Pages projects share the `maxto.github.io` origin, so unchanged storage keys would share progress/preferences across courses. Give this course its own storage namespace. None of these issues requires changing the chosen engine approach.

**H. MEDIUM · PARTLY ADDRESSED: preserving the PDF is conditional on its audit.** (§3 line 87, §5.5 line 188, §8.) “PDF stays” is stronger than “rights have not been checked”. Keep the legacy entry point, but make continued publication/attribution conditional on the review, without silently applying the new content licence to third-party material. The design can plan that audit now and retain the stated owner approval for licensing. No rename or publication should be performed during this review.
